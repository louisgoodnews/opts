## 🎯 Objective

The bus rattles inside where the road is rough: setts, broken asphalt and patches, concrete slabs, kerbs. OMSI does not play rattles by itself: the vehicles bring them. A rattle script (`klappern.osc`, 84 scripts in 37 vehicles, among them the MAN NL/NG/SD200, MB O530, C2, Lion's City and the HH109 buses) turns the change of the body's acceleration into a volume (`klappern_vol`: the jump of `sqrt(0.01·(A_Trans_X² + A_Trans_Y²) + A_Trans_Z²)` over the last frame times `klappern_faktor`, decaying with time), and the sound file plays a rattle loop (`klappern3.wav`, `achsklappern_loop.wav`) with that volume and the speed as `[volcurve]`s. The sounds already played (untriggered loops with script-variable curves, M-0102); what was missing is what moves the body.

---

## 📦 Deliverables & Tasks

- [x] **`A_Trans_Z`** was 0. It is now the vertical acceleration of the chassis following the ground (slopes, kerbs: `ChassisState::ground_vertical_acc_m_s2`, from the elevation in `follow_ground`, bounded at 30 m/s²) plus that of the body on its springs (`BodySuspension::vertical_acc_m_s2`, the mean heave acceleration of the step). Checked: before, driving the HH109 at 23 km/h left `klappern_vol` below 0.05 (the curve is full at 0.2).
- [x] **The road under every wheel.** A street texture's `.surf` bitmap (red channel, mid-grey level, white +2 cm, black −2 cm) is the fine height of the road. The spline profile's ribbons give each lateral strip its texture, `u` across and `v` per metre along (`road_surf::TexStrip`); under each wheel the strip at the road's surface is sampled at the wheel's place along the spline, averaged over a 12 cm contact patch, and added to that wheel's spring input (`wheel_ground_m`). The maps are found beside the texture (`<texture>.surf`, also under the other image extensions) in the profile's texture folder, the map's, the installation's `Texture`, read once per file (`road_surf_load`).
- [x] **Textures without a `.surf`** get a synthetic, deterministic height map from their surface type (`[surface]` of `<texture>.cfg`, asphalt when absent): asphalt 2 mm, concrete 3 mm, interlocking pavers 5 mm, small setts 8 mm, setts 12 mm, gravel 10 mm, dirt, sand, grass and mud 15–20 mm, laid in metres (`road_surf::roughness_of`). **OPTS's assumption ❓**: what OMSI does for such textures is not documented. Surface plates (junctions, squares) and the terrain carry no texture in the ground index and get the asphalt roughness.
- [x] **Kerbs** were already part of the ground height (the profile's surface, M-0090); climbing one lifts the chassis, which now reaches `A_Trans_Z`.
- [x] Tests: decoding a `.surf`, repeating and interpolating, the strip at the road's surface (not the kerb face), the contact patch smoothing ridges, the synthetic maps per surface type, reading `[surface]`, the `.surf` names.

## ✅ Acceptance Criteria

- [x] Berlin-Spandau (spline textures with authored `.surf`: setts `str_kopfgr`, slabs `str_betonplatten`, broken asphalt `str_asphdrk_bad`), MAN NG272 at 13 km/h: `A_Trans_Z` up to ±2.3 m/s², `klappern_vol` 0.3 to 0.97 (checked 2026-10-26).
- [x] Hamburg 109 at Rathausmarkt (surface plates), HH109 at 20 km/h: light rattling, `A_Trans_Z` about ±0.1 m/s², `klappern_vol` up to 0.04.
- [x] Standing still gives no rattle (the wheels sample the same place).

## ❓ Open

- The rattle's loudness against OMSI is unverified: the amplitudes of the synthetic maps and the 12 cm contact patch are tuned by reading the variables, not by ear against OMSI.
- The textures of surface plates (`[surface]` objects: 647 `.surf` beside scenery-object textures, e.g. `str_k_kopfstein.bmp.surf`) and of the terrain are not looked up yet; they rattle like asphalt.
- The `[surface]` type's other effects (grip, tyre sound, force feedback) are not part of this milestone.
